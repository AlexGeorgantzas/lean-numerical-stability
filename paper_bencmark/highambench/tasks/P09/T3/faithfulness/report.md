## Decision

The dependency inventory is semantically clear, and the Lean theorem is a nonvacuous finite-dimensional RMS triangle-budget lemma. Its pointwise sum and normalized Euclidean norm can model equation (4.2) after all propagated error terms and scales have already been supplied. However, the proposition omits the multidimensional FFT, exact/computed quantities, stage construction, one-dimensional K constants, floating-point model, output-RMS linkage, input condition, and quadratic remainder semantics. Those omissions are the substantive content connecting equation (4.2) to Theorem 2(a). Conversely, the paper's FFT-specific theorem does not assert the Lean theorem for arbitrary real vectors and arbitrary budgets. Therefore neither implication direction holds and the appropriate classification is not-faithful-different.

## Implications

- **Lean implies paper:** `no`. The Lean proposition does not derive hdecomp or hlocal from a multidimensional FFT, identify K with K(N_l,gamma), bind yRms to the exact output, or constrain the summed remainder to O(epsilon^2). Recovering Theorem 2(a) requires supplying precisely these missing paper-specific facts externally, so the proposition itself does not imply the selected result.
- **Paper implies lean:** `no`. The paper establishes its inequality only for errors generated by the specified complex multidimensional FFT and floating-point model. It does not assert the Lean proposition's universal conditional statement for arbitrary real vectors, arbitrary K and remainder functions, arbitrary signs, or degenerate cardinalities. The generic triangle lemma is independently true, not a proposition implied by the selected FFT result.

## Findings

- **critical / algorithm-linkage:** The target proves only a generic norm-budget implication and does not establish the benchmark's algorithmic roundoff claim.
- **critical / higher-order-terms:** The conclusion can carry an arbitrarily large or unrelated budget and cannot be read as the paper's asymptotic estimate.
- **major / error-and-scale-linkage:** Even the quantities on the two sides of the Lean inequality need not denote the paper's error or reference scale.
- **major / dimensions-and-norms:** A plausible flattening can be supplied externally, but the proposition does not state the representation needed to match the paper.
- **critical / algorithm-linkage:** It formalizes a generic accumulation lemma rather than the selected algorithmic roundoff theorem.
- **major / error-and-output-semantics:** The translated conclusion cannot be read as the paper's relative forward-error guarantee.
- **major / domain-and-norm:** The paper's multidimensional complex RMS semantics and coordinate-transform scaling are missing.
- **major / numerical-model-and-constants:** The first-order coefficient has the right summation syntax but none of its source meaning or justification.
- **major / higher-order-terms:** The conclusion does not retain the paper's first-order accuracy statement.
- **minor / nonvacuity:** The translated domain includes unintended trivial instances, though this is not the primary reason for rejection.
