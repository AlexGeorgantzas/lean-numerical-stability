## Decision

The verified PDF context resolves the no-overflow issue: it is a standing Section 4 assumption. The declaration exactly reproduces equation (18) only after replacing the paper's floating-point execution and range hypotheses with arbitrary real decompositions and assumptions that force the difficult residual operations to be exact. Its broader quantifiers are therefore not genuine, nonvacuous theorem strength. Since it neither proves the source algorithm satisfies its antecedents nor follows as a universal statement from Lemma 4, both implication directions fail and the appropriate classification is not-faithful-different.

## Implications

- **Lean implies paper:** `no`. Applying the Lean theorem to the paper's procedure would first require an execution link and proofs that the actual outputs satisfy the three TwoProduct decompositions, exact-subtraction representability premises, and final-sum representability premise. Those are the principal numerical obligations proved on printed pp. 396-397, and the declaration supplies none of them.
- **Paper implies lean:** `no`. Lemma 4 concerns outputs generated by ThreeProduct in the particular floating-point system F under the standing no-overflow and stated no-underflow-error conditions. It does not establish the declaration's universal conditional for every predicate on the reals and every freely constructed real trace.

## Findings

- **critical / derived-exactness-assumed:** The paper's central numerical argument is reduced to real-algebra bookkeeping.

- **major / floating-point-contract-omitted:** The source's range-dependent floating-point claim cannot be recovered from the formal statement.
- **major / two-product-semantics-omitted:** Traces that could not arise from TwoProduct satisfy the declaration.
