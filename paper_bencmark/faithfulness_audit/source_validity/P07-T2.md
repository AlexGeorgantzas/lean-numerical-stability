## Published claims

Equation (3.9) assumes

```text
zHat = (YHat + DeltaYHat)^dagger (b + deltaB).
```

The theorem then claims

```text
(A + DeltaA) xHat = b + deltaB.
```

Its proof additionally identifies

```text
xHat = (RHat + DeltaRHat)^(-1)
         (YHat + DeltaYHat)^dagger (b + deltaB)
     = (A + DeltaA)^dagger (b + deltaB),
```

where `A + DeltaA` is the product of the two perturbed factors.

## Defect

The exact perturbed-system equation does not follow from a pseudoinverse
solution unless `b + deltaB` lies in the range of `A + DeltaA`. The paper gives
no such condition or bound on `deltaB`.

The product rule used for the second equality is also false for a general
rank-deficient left factor and a general invertible right factor. Equation
(3.9) places no norm restriction on `DeltaYHat`; hence the paper's earlier
full-rank result for `YHat` does not ensure that `YHat + DeltaYHat` remains full
column rank.

## Counterexamples

For the exact-solution claim, let

```text
Y = [1; 0], R = [1], bTilde = [0; 1].
```

Then `Y` has full column rank, `Y^dagger = [1, 0]`, equation (3.9) permits
`zHat = 0`, and the final triangular solve gives `xHat = 0`. Nevertheless,
`(Y R)xHat = [0; 0]`, not `bTilde`. Thus even full rank does not replace the
missing range condition.

For the product-pseudoinverse identity, let

```text
Y = [[1, 0], [0, 0], [0, 0]],
R = [[1, 1], [0, 1]].
```

Here `R` is invertible, but

```text
(Y R)^dagger = [[1/2, 0, 0], [1/2, 0, 0]],
R^(-1) Y^dagger = [[1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]].
```

The two matrices differ. This is precisely the rank-deficient case that the
unrestricted `DeltaYHat` leaves open.

## Project correction

P07-T2 adds exactly two assumptions:

1. `YHat + DeltaYHat` has full column rank; and
2. `b + deltaB` lies in the range of `A + DeltaA`.

The first validates the proof's product-pseudoinverse identity because the
perturbed triangular factor has a two-sided inverse. The second makes the
Moore--Penrose range projection reproduce the perturbed right-hand side, so
the exact equation printed in Theorem 3.5 follows. These are sufficient repair
conditions identified by this project, not printed source hypotheses.

All other selected content remains unchanged: the Algorithm 1.3 execution
linkage, equation (3.9), equation (2.6), the exact four-term `DeltaA`, the mixed
product, and the immediate page-920 spectral estimate.

## Benchmark and audit decision

The corrected statement remains useful as a benchmark task because proving the
product is a Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse, transferring the range condition to
the computed solution, and assembling the error budget require substantive
formal reasoning. It is not literally equivalent to Theorem 3.5 as printed.
The project therefore retains it as a corrected-source benchmark task but
excludes it from ordinary faithfulness acceptance and from faithfulness-rate
aggregates.

## Thesis-ready summary

Theorem 3.5 combines two claims that require conditions absent from the
published statement. A Moore--Penrose least-squares solution need not solve a
tall perturbed system exactly when the perturbed right-hand side is outside the
matrix range, and the proof's pseudoinverse product rule can fail when the
perturbed LSQR matrix loses column rank. P07-T2 therefore retains the theorem's
algorithmic construction and error bound while explicitly assuming perturbed
full column rank and right-hand-side consistency. Because these are
project-supplied corrections rather than an author-issued erratum, the task is
kept as a benchmark problem but excluded from ordinary faithfulness scoring.
