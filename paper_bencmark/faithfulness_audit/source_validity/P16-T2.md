## Published claim

The independent backward-error branch of Lemma 4.2 assumes the residual and
update models (4.1)-(4.3) and the correction-residual bound (4.14). It concludes

```text
||b - A xHat_(i+1)||_2
  lesssim w_i ||b - A xHat_i||_2
       + (epsilonR + epsilonU + omega_i)
           (||b||_2 + ||A||_F ||xHat_(i+1)||_2).
```

Here `lesssim` means that negligible second-order terms have been dropped.

## Defect in the proof

After exact identity (4.18), the proof bounds the residual-computation term by

```text
epsilonR (||b||_2 + ||A||_F ||xHat_i||_2).
```

To replace the current-iterate norm by the next-iterate norm used in (4.15),
the proof explicitly says it is "considering"

```text
||xHat_i||_2 lesssim ||xHat_(i+1)||_2.
```

Lemma 4.2 does not state this comparison, and it does not follow from (4.1),
(4.2), or (4.14). Without it, the current iterate can be arbitrarily large
while the next iterate is zero.

## Counterexample

For each `e` with `0 < e < 1`, consider the one-dimensional system and
computed step

```text
A = 1,                    b = 1,
xHat_i = 1/e - 1,         xHat_(i+1) = 0,
correctionHat_i = 1 - 1/e,
deltaR_i = -1,            deltaX_i = 0,
rHat_i = 1 - 1/e,
epsilonR = e,             epsilonU = 0,
w_i = 0,                  omega_i = 0.
```

The matrix is nonsingular and the right-hand side is nonzero. The residual
equation is exact:

```text
rHat_i = b - A xHat_i + deltaR_i = 1 - 1/e.
```

Moreover,

```text
|deltaR_i|
  = 1
  = e (|b| + |A| |xHat_i|),
```

so (4.1) holds with equality. The update equation gives
`xHat_(i+1) = xHat_i + correctionHat_i + deltaX_i = 0`, and (4.2) holds.
Finally,

```text
rHat_i - A correctionHat_i = 0,
```

so (4.14) holds with its right-hand side equal to zero.

Equation (4.15) would nevertheless require

```text
1 lesssim e
```

as `e -> 0`. The omitted discrepancy is order one, not second order.
Therefore the published hypotheses do not imply the displayed recurrence
under the paper's stated first-order convention.

## Project correction

P16-T2 adds the comparison used by the proof as an explicit hypothesis:

```text
||xHat_i||_2 lesssim ||xHat_(i+1)||_2.
```

The computed-step structure now records only the printed operation and
correction models. The project correction appears directly in the theorem
signature, so it cannot be mistaken for a consequence or hidden certificate
field.

Because the paper gives no quantitative definition of `lesssim`, the task
uses an explicit benchmark semantics: along a nonbottom filter, the left side
is eventually bounded by the right side plus an `O(scale^2)` remainder, with
`scale -> 0`. This semantics is also project supplied.

Under that hypothesis, the proof following (4.18) is valid. Multiplying the
iterate-comparison remainder by the eventually bounded nonnegative
`epsilonR` and the fixed Frobenius norm of `A` preserves second-order
size, yielding (4.15).

## Benchmark and audit decision

The corrected task remains useful because it requires exact residual
composition, Euclidean triangle inequalities, a Frobenius matrix-action bound,
the three error sources in (4.15), and second-order remainder propagation. No
mounted frozen declaration proves this combination.

The added comparison is not a printed hypothesis or an author-issued erratum,
and the filter-level interpretation of `lesssim` is not uniquely fixed by the
paper. P16-T2 is therefore retained as a corrected-source benchmark task but
excluded from ordinary faithfulness acceptance and faithfulness-rate
aggregates.

## Thesis-ready summary

The backward-error branch of Lemma 4.2 invokes a comparison between the
current and next iterate norms that is absent from the lemma's hypotheses. A
one-dimensional family satisfies the printed residual, update, and correction
models while keeping the next iterate at zero and the next residual at one; in
that family the claimed first-order recurrence would reduce to
`1 lesssim epsilonR` as `epsilonR` tends to zero.

P16-T2 therefore retains Lemma 4.2 as its source but makes the missing
iterate-norm comparison explicit. The corrected theorem still derives exact
identity (4.18) and recurrence (4.15), with second-order terms represented by
an explicit filter-level remainder. Since both the added condition and that
precise remainder semantics are project supplied, the task is kept for
benchmarking but excluded from ordinary paper-faithfulness scoring.
