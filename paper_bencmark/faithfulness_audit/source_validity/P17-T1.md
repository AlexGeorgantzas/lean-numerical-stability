## Published claim

Theorem 3.6 considers random errors
`delta_1, ..., delta_n` and truncation biases `beta_1, ..., beta_n`. Its
hypotheses identify the mean of `delta_1` with that of `beta_1`, identify each
later conditional mean with `beta_k`, and require `|beta_k| <= B`. It concludes

```text
(1 - B)^n <= E[product k=1..n (1 + delta_k)] <= (1 + B)^n.
```

The printed hypotheses do not require `1 + delta_k` or the accumulated partial
products to be nonnegative, and they do not require `B <= 1`.

## Defect in the proof

Writing `Q_k = product j=1..k (1 + delta_j)`, the proof obtains

```text
E[Q_n] = E[Q_(n-1) (1 + beta_n)].
```

It then bounds this expression by replacing `1 + beta_n` with `1 - B` and
`1 + B`. This multiplication preserves the inequality only when `Q_(n-1)` is
nonnegative almost surely. That condition is not among the printed hypotheses.
The lower induction step also uses multiplication by `1 - B`, which requires
`B <= 1`.

A sufficient repair is:

```text
delta_k >= -1 almost surely for every k, and 0 <= B <= 1.
```

These are sufficient repair conditions identified by this project, not printed
hypotheses of Theorem 3.6.

## Counterexample

Take a probability space with two equally likely outcomes `a` and `b`, and set
`n = 2` and `B = 1/2`:

| Variable | Outcome `a` | Outcome `b` |
|---|---:|---:|
| `delta_1` | `10` | `-10` |
| `beta_1` | `0` | `0` |
| `delta_2` | `1/2` | `-1/2` |
| `beta_2` | `1/2` | `-1/2` |

Then:

- `E[delta_1] = 0 = E[beta_1]`;
- `delta_1` distinguishes the two outcomes, so
  `E[delta_2 | delta_1] = delta_2 = beta_2`;
- `|beta_1| <= B` and `|beta_2| <= B` everywhere.

Thus all printed hypotheses hold. However,

```text
E[(1 + delta_1)(1 + delta_2)]
  = (1/2) * (11 * 3/2) + (1/2) * (-9 * 1/2)
  = 6,
```

while

```text
(1 + B)^2 = (3/2)^2 = 9/4.
```

Therefore the printed upper bound would require `6 <= 9/4`, which is false.

## Relation to Remark 3.7

Remark 3.7 specializes the product bound to errors generated by
`SR_(p,r)` and concludes equation (3.4) with `B = u_(p+r)`. The surrounding
source context supplies facts absent from the abstract Theorem 3.6:

- a p-bit rounding error satisfies `|delta_k| < u_p`;
- with positive precision, `u_p <= 1`, so `delta_k > -1` and every factor
  `1 + delta_k` is positive;
- the truncation bias satisfies `|beta_k| <= u_(p+r)`;
- positive `p` and `r` give `0 < u_(p+r) < 1`; and
- Lemma 3.2 supplies the required conditional-expectation relation.

Consequently, the sign conditions needed by the induction are consequences of
the actual stochastic-rounding model. Equation (3.4) is therefore a sound
source-based resolution of the mathematical defect.

P17-T1 does not select Remark 3.7 because the project owner chose to retain the
abstract Theorem 3.6 and correct its missing hypotheses directly. Remark 3.7
still confirms that those sign conditions hold in the paper's intended
stochastic-rounding application.

## Thesis-ready summary

During source validation, we found that Theorem 3.6 of El Arar et al. admits a
counterexample as written. Its induction bounds a product by multiplying
factorwise inequalities, but the theorem does not ensure that the preceding
partial product is nonnegative. On a two-outcome probability space, random
variables can satisfy all printed mean, conditional-mean, and bias-bound
hypotheses while the expected two-factor product equals `6`, exceeding the
claimed upper bound `9/4` for `B = 1/2`.

The paper's intended limited-precision stochastic-rounding specialization in
Remark 3.7 avoids the defect because its rounding model makes every factor
`1 + delta_k` positive and places `u_(p+r)` below one. We rebuilt P17-T1 as a
project-corrected version of Theorem 3.6: it retains the probabilistic variables,
conditional means, and expected-product conclusion while explicitly assuming
nonnegative factors and `0 < B <= 1`. This makes the mathematical correction
transparent, but it also removes the former direct-use rationale; the tier must
be reconsidered before benchmark measurement.
