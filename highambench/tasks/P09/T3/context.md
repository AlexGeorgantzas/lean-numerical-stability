## Arrays, norms, and exact transforms

`P09MultidimensionalFftPlan` has `m > 0` axes. Each axis has positive length
`N_l` and a certified one-dimensional mixed-radix FFT plan. Arrays are complex
functions on the product of the cyclic coordinate index sets. `p09MultiRms` is
the complex Euclidean norm divided by `sqrt(N_1 * ... * N_m)`, matching the
normalization immediately before Theorem 2.

`p09CoordinateTransform` is the positive-sign unnormalized DFT in one
coordinate. `p09ApplyCoordinatePrefix axis m` applies `T_m` first and `T_1`
last, matching the nested order

```text
T_1(T_2(...T_m(X))).
```

The plan records equation (4.4)'s exact RMS scaling for every prefix.

## Operational computation

`p09RoundedCoordinateTransform` applies the axis's mixed-radix plan to every
coordinate fiber. It invokes the same rounded trigonometric roots, complex
products, and recursive sums used by the operational one-dimensional task.

`P09MultidimensionalFftRun` contains no freely chosen local-error arrays. Its
last state is the exactly represented input, and every preceding state is
generated by `p09RoundedCoordinateTransform`. `p09AxisLocalError` is then
defined as the generated state minus the corresponding exact coordinate
transform. Consequently the local-error decomposition and the final
telescoping relation are mathematical consequences of the trace rather than
certificate fields.

The Wilkinson model is real-valued and does not add overflow, underflow,
subnormal, NaN, or infinity semantics. This is the scope of the source model.

## Uniform asymptotic family

Theorem 2(a) contains a first-order term plus `O(epsilon^2)`. The structure
`P09AsymptoticMultidimensionalFftFamily` therefore fixes the plan, input, and
common trigonometric constant `gamma` before precision varies. For every
positive `epsilon`, it supplies a Wilkinson model with exactly that precision
and a linked operational run on the same input.

The input is exactly represented in every run. This is the explicit setting
identified in section 6. The sentence on printed page 765 that also permits an
`O(epsilon)` input-RMS change is a possible extension of the proof; it is not
used as an unlinked alternative input certificate here.

## Derived one-axis analysis

The target accepts no local-error bound or theorem certificate from its
caller. `p09PropagatedAxisErrorRms_le_operations` constructs the
one-dimensional trace of each coordinate fiber from the same rounded roots,
products, and sums used by `p09RoundedCoordinateTransform`. The operation-level
proof of Theorem 1 then gives an explicit estimate for every fiber, and a
finite-product norm argument combines the fibers. Equation (4.4) propagates
that estimate through the preceding exact coordinate transforms.

For each coordinate and `epsilon <= 1`, the derived estimate has the form

```text
(epsilon * K(N_l, gamma) + B_l * epsilon^2)
  * RMS(propagated computed stage input),
```

where `B_l` is an explicit nonnegative constant determined only by the fixed
axis plan and `gamma`. The proof of the target derives the telescoping identity,
bounds the generated intermediate states, solves the resulting small-epsilon
recurrence, and absorbs the quadratic products into one coefficient fixed
before `epsilon`.

## Target conclusion

The exact-output RMS is positive because the printed theorem divides by
`Y_RMS` without specifying a zero-output convention. The target derives one
nonnegative coefficient `C` and one positive radius, both chosen before
`epsilon`, such that throughout that right neighborhood

```text
RMS(fl(Y) - Y) / RMS(Y)
  <= epsilon * sum_l K(N_l, gamma) + C * epsilon^2.
```

The proof derives the finite telescoping estimate from the generated states,
controls all computed intermediate RMS values from the local estimates, and
absorbs only genuinely higher-order products into the uniform coefficient.
